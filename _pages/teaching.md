I teach engineering as a way to connect fundamental principles with consequential decisions about energy, infrastructure, and the environment. Across courses, mentoring, and research training, I aim to help students build physical intuition, quantitative confidence, and the ability to reason clearly about open-ended problems.

## Teaching philosophy

Students learn most deeply when they can see both how a physical system works and why an engineering decision matters in practice. I therefore structure learning around a progression from foundational concepts, to quantitative analysis, to open-ended applications. My role is to make that progression rigorous and accessible: establish clear expectations, offer multiple ways to engage with difficult material, and gradually give students greater ownership of problems, methods, and conclusions.

In fluids, heat transfer, geomechanics, and subsurface systems, I use real engineering cases to make abstract ideas tangible. Students might apply conservation laws and simple numerical models to geothermal heat extraction, carbon-storage containment, or infrastructure reuse, then interpret the implications for performance, uncertainty, safety, and environmental impact. This approach develops both physical intuition and practical analytical skills.

## Inclusive teaching and research training

I create inclusive learning environments by making expectations, evaluation criteria, and paths to participation transparent. For complex assignments, I use staged deliverables and early feedback so students can identify and address difficulties before high-stakes assessment. I combine individual reflection, small-group discussion, and full-class engagement to support students with different preparation and communication styles. Accessible example notebooks, documented computational workflows, and explicit discussion of assumptions help students build confidence with quantitative tools.

I apply the same principles in research mentoring. New students benefit from clear onboarding, achievable early milestones, and regular individual and group meetings. I create well-scoped entry projects for undergraduates and students from adjacent disciplines, while progressively supporting greater technical and intellectual independence. My goal is a research environment in which students can ask questions openly, learn from mistakes, and see a clear path for growth.
