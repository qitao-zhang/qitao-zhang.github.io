My research examines the subsurface as an essential part of sustainable energy infrastructure. Across geothermal energy, carbon storage, and underground energy storage, I investigate how coupled physical processes influence efficiency, reliability, and long-term performance.

## Geothermal energy and adaptive flow regulation

Uneven flow through fracture networks can cause injected water to return rapidly to production wells, bypassing much of the available heat. My work examines the onset and evolution of this thermal short-circuiting and explores ways to distribute flow more effectively.

I investigate temperature-responsive proppants, downhole flow-control strategies, and thermally activated cellular screens (TACS). Coupled thermo-hydro-mechanical–wellbore models connect fracture evolution, heat transfer, reservoir flow, and wellbore conditions to evaluate how these approaches can sustain heat extraction.

**Key questions:** How do cooling and stress redistribution change fracture flow? Can adaptive flow regulation delay thermal breakthrough and extend geothermal system lifetimes?

## Infrastructure reuse and underground energy storage

Abandoned oil and gas wells, underground mines, and mine-water systems offer opportunities to reuse existing infrastructure for clean energy. My research explores their use for geothermal heating, long-duration underground energy storage, and low-carbon data-center cooling.

This work considers the interaction between thermal resources, flow pathways, and operating strategies to assess when infrastructure reuse can deliver useful energy services.

**Key questions:** Which existing subsurface assets can support heat recovery or energy storage? How can their thermal and hydraulic behavior guide efficient operation?

## Geologic carbon storage and geomechanical integrity

Reliable geologic CO₂ storage requires understanding where injected CO₂ travels and how injection affects reservoir and fault stability. My work combines subsurface-flow modeling, geomechanical analysis, and physics-informed machine learning to investigate plume evolution and containment in heterogeneous reservoirs.

**Key questions:** How can physical constraints improve prediction of CO₂ plume evolution? How do reservoir heterogeneity and stress changes affect storage performance and fault-reactivation risk?

## Methods

I use coupled-process simulation, computational geomechanics, and scientific computing to connect fracture-scale behavior with reservoir-scale performance. My experience includes finite-element and finite-volume methods, fracture modeling, reservoir–wellbore coupling, and physics-informed data-driven approaches.
